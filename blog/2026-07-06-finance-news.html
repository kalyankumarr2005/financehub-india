Indian Markets Scale New Peaks: Sensex and Nifty Rally, FPIs Return with Vigor

The Indian stock market witnessed a remarkable session today, July 6, 2026, with benchmark indices Sensex and Nifty 50 closing at their highest levels in ten weeks. The bullish momentum was fueled by a significant return of Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), optimism surrounding a pivotal IPO, and a generally positive global sentiment. Market Summary: A Day of Strong Gains and Positive Sentiment

Today's trading session was characterized by broad-based buying across sectors, pushing the key indices significantly higher:

  • Sensex: The BSE Sensex surged 521 points, or 0.67%, to close at a robust 78,285.07.
  • Nifty 50: The NSE Nifty 50 followed suit, climbing 160 points, or 0.66%, to settle comfortably above the 24,400 mark at 24,430.
  • Broader Markets: The positive sentiment extended beyond large-caps, with the Nifty Midcap 100 gaining 0.45% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 rising an impressive 0.75%, indicating a healthy market breadth.

A key catalyst for this rally was the strong inflow of foreign capital. Foreign Institutional Investors (FPIs) injected a substantial ₹14,109 crore into Indian equities in the second half of June, signaling a renewed appetite for Indian assets. This inflow was predominantly concentrated in financial services, suggesting FPIs are betting on the robust health of India's banking and financial sector. The moderation of selling pressure in FMCG and IT sectors further contributed to the overall positive sentiment.

Globally, US stock futures, particularly the Nasdaq 100, also saw gains of up to 1%, driven by a recovery in tech stocks and declining crude oil prices. This positive global backdrop, coupled with receding concerns over immediate Federal Reserve rate hikes, provided additional tailwinds for the Indian markets.

The Rupee's Dance: Global Pressures and Local Strengths

While equities soared, the Indian Rupee (INR) experienced a slight depreciation against the US Dollar today, closing at 95.38 (provisional), down 20 paise from its previous close. This movement was primarily due to a strengthening greenback, which weighed on emerging market currencies globally. However, the significant gains in local equities and the continued fall in international crude oil prices (following OPEC+'s decision to raise output) helped cap the downside for the INR. Optimism surrounding the upcoming corporate earnings season and strong provisional June quarter business growth updates from banks also provided underlying support, preventing a sharper decline.

NSE IPO: A Mega Listing on the Horizon

Dominating headlines and investor discussions is the impending Initial Public Offering (IPO) of the National Stock Exchange (NSE). This much-anticipated public issue is set to be a landmark event for the Indian capital markets:

  • Target Launch: The NSE is targeting a September 2026 launch for its IPO.
  • Estimated Size: The mega IPO is estimated at a colossal ₹30,000 crore ($3 billion), potentially valuing the country's largest stock exchange at over ₹5 lakh crore.
  • Market Impact: If successful, this issue will surpass Hyundai Motor India's ₹27,870-crore public offering launched in October 2024, making it one of India's largest-ever IPOs.
  • Structure: The proposed public issue will be entirely an Offer For Sale (OFS) of 14.89 crore equity shares. Existing shareholders, including major entities like State Bank of India and MS Strategic (Mauritius) Ltd, will collectively divest nearly 6% of the exchange's equity.
  • Marketing Commencement: Formal marketing for the IPO is expected to begin as early as next week, with roadshows planned to engage potential investors. The NSE has already appointed 20 banks to manage this monumental sale.

The NSE IPO represents a rare opportunity for public investors to own a stake in the backbone of India's equity market infrastructure. Its success will not only provide liquidity to existing shareholders but also further deepen and mature the Indian capital markets.
